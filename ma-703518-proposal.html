The meter

You are charged for packages, not for people

The monthly fee is metered on tender packages run through the system. Coordinators, project managers, construction managers, estimators — as many as you like, at no extra cost.

Why seats are free

Because the number of people logged in does not move our cost by a dollar. What costs money is work done: packages issued, replies read, quotes levelled. Charging per seat would price you out of putting the whole team on it, which is the one thing that makes the method stick.

What counts as a package

One trade package on one project, from invitation to either an award or a decision not to proceed. Div 09 drywall on Stoneridge is one package, however many trades were invited to it and however many follow-ups it took. Re-tendering the same scope after a redesign counts again.

Overage
$150 per package beyond the monthly allowance, billed in arrears. If you run over three months running, we move you to the next tier rather than keep billing overage — that is a worse deal for you and we would rather you noticed it from us than from an invoice.

The question in the deck

BuildingConnected — use what you have, or build your own?

Aaron's deck ends on this and it is the right place to end. Mosaic pays roughly $14,000 a year for a platform that already sends invitations, tracks who is bidding, distributes drawings, runs custom bid forms and levels bids side by side. That spend happens whether or not anyone uses it.

The case for the platform

Purpose-built, prequalification included, Autodesk maintains it, nobody internal writing anything. The trade-off is that you work to its process, and your Appendix C scopes have to fit its bid forms.

The case for the agent layer

It works the way Mosaic already tenders — your Appendix C, your cost codes, your documents. It carries on past the award into recommendations and regret letters. The trade-off is that you own the upkeep.

What we actually think

It is probably both. BuildingConnected for solicitation and prequalification; the agent layer for levelling against your own scopes and for everything that happens after the award. Neither option here asks you to cancel it.

Answer the prior question first
Before either option, somebody should establish whether BuildingConnected failed in practice or was never properly started — is the trade list loaded and current, do the Appendix C scopes translate into its bid forms, do the trades actually use it. That is a week of somebody's attention, it costs nothing, and it changes what you should buy. We would rather you did that before signing anything with us. If the answer is that the platform works and nobody switched it on, the pilot is still worth running — but for the levelling, not the chasing.

Plan tier

Team for the pilot, with an Enterprise review once usage is real. The trigger to move up is IT wanting automated on/offboarding or leadership wanting a usage audit trail — not capability. The models are the same on both.

And what IT will want to see
Business plans are covered by Anthropic's commercial terms rather than the consumer ones. IT should review the current data handling and retention terms and sign off on the connector scope before any project data is loaded. The scope is deliberately small: one mailbox, project tender folders on SharePoint, one read-only Excel workbook for budget lines, and a Teams channel. No Sage, no Timberline, no payroll, no banking — and no path to any of them.
