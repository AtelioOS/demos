01 — What we saw

Three companies that are really one operation.

Before building anything we looked at how the three businesses actually fit together. Three things stood out, and they shaped everything that follows.

One yard

4241 Barnsdale Road

All three companies work out of the same place, share machines and share crews. They are not a portfolio of separate investments — they are one operation with three sets of books.

One site, three companies

Grade → seed → irrigate

On a subdivision, Lanthier grades and supplies topsoil, Ottawa Hydroseeding seeds it, Premier installs irrigation. Same customer, same site, three schedules that only exist in people's heads.

One clock

Frost-out to freeze-up

Roughly late April to mid-November. Everything is compressed into it, and the autumn winterisation round is the tightest part of the whole year.

The problem is not that you have three dashboards.

It is that the baton gets dropped between your own companies. A job stalls because a grade certificate is unsigned, so seeding cannot start, so irrigation slips — and nobody is late to a customer, so nobody escalates. That is invisible in three separate systems and obvious in one. The demo you have been through leads with exactly this, because we think it is the single largest recoverable loss in the group.

02 — The approach

Answering the three things you asked for.

You asked for

Running multiple businesses

One workspace, one login, a company switch that re-computes every screen. Each entity keeps its own invoices, its own HST number and its own books — because it has to — and you get a group view on top with the money you charge yourself taken back out.

You asked for

Projects moving forward

Every job on one board with a stage and an age. A dedicated view of everything that has stopped, ranked by what the delay is costing. Multi-company sites shown as a chain so it is always clear whose turn it is.

You asked for

Clear KPIs

The same six measures for all three companies, weekly, against target — plus the operational numbers each business actually runs on: yards delivered, systems installed, area seeded, germination pass rate, callback rate.

Two principles we would hold to

Nothing is true unless the crew enters it. Job costing, invoicing and the customer portal are all downstream of a foreman tapping a phone in a truck. So the field app gets designed first and kept to five taps and no typing. Every field system that fails, fails here — not in the office.

We do not replace your accountant. The system runs operations and hands clean numbers to QuickBooks. We do not do your general ledger, we do not run payroll, and we do not file your returns.

03 — Phasing

The order is set by your calendar, not ours.

A landscape and irrigation group cannot absorb a new operating system in July. It can in January. And there are two dates each year that cannot move. We would sequence the build around both.

Now – mid Sep
Peak season. Nobody has time.
Sep 21 – Nov 15
Winterisation. ~612 visits, 8 weeks.
Dec – Feb
The only real window for change.
Mar – Apr
Rehire, recertify, spring start-ups.
May onward
Peak again. Must already work.

Dates are the typical Ottawa season; we would confirm against your own records.

Phase 1
Before Sep 21
Hard deadline — set by freeze-up, not by us

The winterisation campaign

Your ~612 winterisation agreements become records instead of a spreadsheet. The system generates the work orders, sends customers a link to book their own day, and sequences each truck's route. Crews close jobs on their phones.

We would start here for one reason: it is the only part of the year where being three weeks late costs you a whole season. Booking pace, not truck capacity, is what pushes the last blow-out past the frost — and that is a scheduling problem software genuinely fixes.

This phase is only possible if we start soon.

There are about five weeks until the campaign opens. If a decision lands quickly, this is achievable and you would feel the benefit this autumn. If it does not, we would not pretend otherwise — Phase 1 becomes the spring start-up round in April instead, and everything below shifts one season. We would rather say that now than discover it in October.

Phase 2
Dec – Feb
The quiet months — when change is actually possible

The core operating layer

The part you saw first in the demo: every job from all three companies on one board, the stalled view, the cross-company handoff chain, quoting on your own unit rates, and the customer portal where a builder signs a grade certificate and releases the next company on the site.

Invoicing comes in here too — including statutory holdback tracking under the Construction Act, with the 60-day release clock watched automatically. On your builder work that is real money nobody currently goes back for.

04 — Built vs new

Most of this already exists. Some of it does not.

We are not proposing to invent an operating system for you. Most of what you saw runs today for other clients in other industries — restaurants, equipment service, manufacturing, a coffee-equipment distributor. 05 — What we would need

From you, to make this real.

Data, roughly in this order

  • Your winterisation and start-up contract list — properties, zone counts, last year's notes
  • QuickBooks customer and item exports for each of the three entities
  • Current rate cards: topsoil by product, hydroseed by area, irrigation by zone
  • Crew list with roles, and the equipment list with any service intervals you track
  • A handful of recent quotes and invoices, so we match how you already price
  • Certification records — who holds what, and when it expires

People and time

  • You, for about an hour a fortnight — decisions, not demos
  • One person who genuinely knows the operation, roughly two hours a week
  • One foreman willing to be the pilot for the crew app, and to be honest about it
  • Whoever does your books, for two conversations about how data reaches QuickBooks

Decisions we would need early

  • What the group workspace is actually called — "Barnsdale Road" is our placeholder
  • Whether crews clock in per job or per day
  • Who is allowed to see group-level financials

Explicitly not in scope

Being clear about this up front tends to save an argument later. We would not build, and would not charge you for:

General ledger

Keep QuickBooks. We push clean data to it.

Payroll

We produce the hours; your payroll provider runs them.

Marketing / CRM funnels

Your growth comes from builder relationships, not campaigns.

Fleet GPS

Worth having; a separate purchase we can integrate later.

06 — Risks

What could go wrong, and what we would do about it.

RiskHow it actually shows upWhat we do about it
Crews do not use it
Highest risk		 The office loves it, the trucks ignore it, and within a month the data is wrong — so the KPIs are wrong, so you stop trusting the whole thing. The field app is designed first, not last. Five taps, no typing, works offline. One crew pilots it for two weeks before anyone else sees it, and if that foreman will not use it we change the app rather than push harder.
The contract data is messier than expected The 612 winterisation records turn out to be a spreadsheet with gaps in zone counts and stale addresses. This is normal, not a criticism. We import what exists, flag the gaps, and let crews correct records from the truck as they visit. The list cleans itself over one season instead of needing a data project first.
The season closes on Phase 1 Decisions take a few weeks, the campaign opens Sep 21, and the window is gone. We say so immediately rather than compressing quality. Phase 1 moves to the spring round and Phase 2 starts in the quiet months as planned — the sequence survives, the calendar shifts.
Three entities, three sets of books Consolidation gets treated as a filing rather than a report, or inter-company charges get counted twice. Each entity's books and HST returns stay entirely separate and are never merged. Consolidation is a read-only view. Your accountant sees the model before it is built, not after.
Scope grows quietly Every phase gains "just one more thing" and the timeline doubles. Each phase has a written definition of done agreed before it starts. New requests go on a list for the next phase by default, not into the current one.
